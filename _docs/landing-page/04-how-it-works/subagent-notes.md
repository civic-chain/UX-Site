# 04 How It Works

## Full Visual Critique

1. A jury would say: neat and on-brand, but too predictable. Evidence: three nearly identical cards sit in a row with identical top illustration zones and similar text structure.
2. In 1-2 seconds, the eye lands on the large heading, then on the card row, then gets stuck on three identical top panels. Evidence: the heaviest elements after the heading are the three large light rectangles inside cards.
3. Meaning: a calm path from request to payout, but visually it looks more like a comparison of three equal functions. Evidence: cards stand horizontally like options, not as movement.
4. The main action, read and trust the process, is understandable, but process direction is weak. Evidence: there is `STEP ONE / TWO / THREE`, but no strong line, arrow, vertical rhythm, or other motion cue.
5. Squint hierarchy: heading first, three colored cards second, internal text third. The hierarchy is clean, but process meaning loses to grid form. Evidence: you see a row of three large blocks, not a `request -> spend -> payout` path.
6. Top-right air reads as empty, while the lower card row feels dense and heavy. Evidence: a large empty area sits right of the heading; below it, three cards fill almost the full width without pause.
7. The section is connected by color and type, but a process block should change rhythm. Evidence: warm paper, navy serif, and soft colors match the system, but the composition is still `three cards in a row`.
8. Top illustration panels are too large for simple icons and two small labels. Evidence: each panel has too much empty space around a small icon and two short pills.
9. There is a mismatch between text density and the large decorative zone at the top of each card. Evidence: the card top occupies almost half the height but carries little information.
10. The interface is clear and readable, but the repeated format is visually tiring. Evidence: every card follows the same `step`, icon, two labels, heading, paragraph structure.
11. Icons and decor are soft, but decorative circles feel mechanically repeated. Evidence: each card has the same large circle in the upper right and a similar lower-left arc.
12. Main edges are aligned, but the composition is too grid-like: catalog, not story. Evidence: all three cards share the same height, top module, and row placement.
13. Internal rhythm is loose in places: the top module is large, while text is compressed lower. Evidence: headings start right after the large panel, and paragraphs already press toward the lower area.
14. The section is not text-heavy, but card lines are long and similar in weight. Evidence: every paragraph takes 4-5 lines and all three blocks feel equally dense.
15. It currently feels closer to template AI design: attractive, but too symmetrical and expected. Evidence: identical cards, decorative spots, no surprising compositional gesture.
16. The block needs to become more precise: not louder in color, clearer in process. Evidence: palette is already expressive; the problem is that the horizontal row does not feel like a path.
17. It violates proximity to meaning: elements look like three equal groups, but should read as one sequence. Evidence: identical card gaps make them independent neighbors.
18. One main fix: stack vertical steps and connect them with a shared process line. Evidence: the horizontal row is the main source of repetition and catalog feeling.

## What To Improve

- Rebuild the three steps vertically: `request`, `controlled spend`, `payout`.
- Add a thin vertical line on the left with `01 / 02 / 03` numbers.
- Make each card a wide editorial panel: number, short phase, heading, text, small icon.
- Reduce decorative top panels or turn them into side accents.
- Keep cream / sky / sage / gold as soft accents without increasing decoration.

## Main Fix

Replace the three-card row with a vertical process of three cards/steps stacked under each other.

Palette: cream, sky, sage, small gold accents; calm, premium, editorial, with no crypto/web3, neon, dark mode, gradient blobs, and no rigid aspect ratio.
