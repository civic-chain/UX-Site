# 01 Hero

## Full Visual Critique

1. A jury would say: strong editorial cover, but not yet an interface hero. Evidence: large serif headline and authored illustration are present, but there is no button or clear action.
2. In 1-2 seconds, the eye lands on `Every dollar...`, then on the official and worker, then stalls in the empty space between copy and illustration. Evidence: the largest navy text is on the left, the tallest figures are on the right, and the center is almost empty.
3. Meaning: transparent public procurement, human trust, money in view. It does this well. Evidence: a businessperson shows a card to a worker, residents stand nearby, and the copy mentions public spending and vendors.
4. The main action is unclear: right now this is a "read and believe" block, not a "click" block. Evidence: no `Contact us`, no button, no email.
5. Squint hierarchy: headline first, two large figures second, small audience columns third. CTA should become the third strong node. Evidence: the lower columns are weaker, and there is room for subtitle plus future button between them.
6. The whitespace mostly works, but the central empty area needs a functional accent. Evidence: between the left subtitle and right illustration there is a lot of light space with no role.
7. The section connects to the landing story: promise, audiences, civic scene. The CTA lacks a "what next" bridge. Evidence: there are `For residents` and `For vendors`, but no next contact step.
8. No strange stretched forms; text columns are tidy. The CTA should not become a wide SaaS slab. Evidence: the composition relies on short lines and open blocks, not cards.
9. The main odd emptiness sits below the subtitle and left of the illustration. Evidence: there is enough room for a button, but currently it does not support the user path.
10. As an interface block it is readable but not obvious; it looks like a poster. Evidence: no interactive element, email, or link, only narrative.
11. The illustration fits beautifully: warm reds, gold, cream, human civic tone. Evidence: paper background, soft figures, no shadows, glare, or tech graphics.
12. Alignment is good: the left text edge is clean and the illustration holds the right mass. CTA should sit on the left grid. Evidence: headline, subtitle, and columns start from one logical left axis.
13. Spacing rhythm is good at the top and between text, but the gap between subtitle and columns is too passive. Evidence: after the subtitle, there is a large vertical interval before `For residents / For vendors`.
14. The section is not text-heavy. Evidence: big headline, short subtitle, two columns of 2-3 lines.
15. The block feels fresh and authored, not like a generic AI design. Evidence: the illustration is narrative and editorial, not abstract SaaS.
16. The block needs to become more precise, not louder. Evidence: style is already expressive; the missing piece is a clear `Contact us` action and `info@civic-chain.com` contact.
17. Best-practice contradiction: no visible primary action and weak connection between promise and next step. Evidence: after the value proposition, the user gets no button or contact.
18. Strongest fix: add an editorial CTA group below the subtitle. Evidence: that is the free space, and it does not conflict with headline, columns, or illustration.

## What To Improve

- Place `Contact us` below the subtitle, before the `For residents` / `For vendors` columns.
- Make the button navy with cream text, a thin gold/cream edge, and a very soft shadow.
- Put `info@civic-chain.com` to the right on desktop and below the button on narrow screens.
- Do not use a card, gradient, glass effect, or .gov-style form.
- Slightly increase CTA weight while keeping the illustration as the main emotional accent.

## Main Fix

Add a calm, visible CTA block, `Contact us` plus `info@civic-chain.com`, in the empty space between subtitle and audience columns.

## Imagegen Brief

Keep the warm civic / GovTech / editorial NYT hero: cream paper grid, navy serif headline, clean sans, and human procurement illustration on the right. Add a visible but restrained navy `Contact us` button below the subtitle, with `info@civic-chain.com` next to it or below it as a refined text link. The style should feel public-sector, warm, and trustworthy, with no crypto/web3, neon, dark mode, gradient blobs, generic SaaS, or bureaucratic .gov form.
