# Customer Savings Mechanism

## What This Document Owns

This is the business-canon answer to **why CivicChain lowers total municipal
vendor-payment cost**. It owns the mechanism. The downstream
[[customer-savings-dollar-model|Customer savings dollar model]] turns the
mechanism into bold planning dollars.

- Claim status: **mechanism is derived business canon**; percentages and ROI
  remain **Modeled** until a pilot measures them.
- Use this before writing landing-page, pitch, investor, pricing, or sales copy
  about savings, fees, payment rails, or ROI.
- The positive claim is not "crypto is cheaper." The positive claim is:
  **CivicChain makes every contract cheaper to pay, control, reconcile, and
  explain.**

## One-line answer

CivicChain lowers total cost by turning municipal payment from a slow,
after-the-fact paperwork problem into a contract-controlled, audit-ready
workflow. Vendors price less uncertainty into bids, clerks spend fewer hours
rebuilding records, out-of-scope spend is caught earlier, and the public record
is produced while the work happens.

The clean unit is **all-in cost per reconciled, audit-ready, legally valid
municipal transaction**.

Narrative guardrail: every vendor, resident, or worker benefit has to return to
municipal ROI. Faster vendor payment is a bidder-pool and risk-premium lever;
public visibility is staff-time, audit, and public-record posture; field-spend
convenience is buyer control and fewer disputes.

## The Four Value Engines

| Value engine | Who feels it | Why it saves money | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payment certainty | Municipality, through vendor behavior | A vendor who trusts the approval and payment workflow needs less bid padding for 30-45 day waits, invoice uncertainty, and cash-flow risk; the buyer payoff is a wider bidder pool and lower modeled risk premium. | **Modeled / Needs proof** until measured in real bids. |
| Record automation | Municipality | SAR, WCR, payment authorization, ledger hash, and dashboard record replace manual invoice chasing, check matching, export cleanup, and month-end reconstruction. | **Modeled**, supported by Maine operating evidence. |
| Spend control before settlement | Municipality | MCC locks, card pool balances, per-card limits, hard declines, and admin-approved refills turn policy into authorization-time control. | **Target** while card v1.0 scope is unresolved. |
| Continuous audit/public evidence | Municipality, with residents and auditors as readers | Each contract assembles its own proof package, reducing FOAA/FOIA response work, audit prep, and "who got paid what?" staff time. | **Modeled / Target**, with Maine FOAA/audit evidence as support. |

Resident card/convenience fees are useful emotional context: people in the U.S.
do see painful fees when paying government. The bigger CivicChain business case
is stronger: the town saves around the vendor-payment workflow, not merely on a
checkout fee.

## The Hidden-Cost Stack CivicChain Compresses

| Layer | Treat as | CivicChain relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment processor / portal fees | Visible cost | CivicChain can replace part of the portal + reconciliation stack for contract spend. |
| Banking and treasury services | Operational cost | Rail/accounts/webhooks can reduce manual treasury handling, matching, and exception research. |
| Settlement delay | Core treated pain | Faster approved settlement and earlier work-expense funding reduce the uncertainty vendors price into bids. |
| Back-office reconciliation | Core treated pain | SAR/WCR/ledger events reduce matching, exports, audit prep, and public-record assembly. |
| Exceptions, research, and returns | Hidden cost | Fewer mismatches, rejected payments, emergency checks, disputes, and manual research hours can matter more than a lower rail fee. |
| Card/contactless acceptance | Buyer control tool | Card economics remain, but the card layer earns its place when it creates spend control, evidence, and fewer reimbursement/receipt disputes. |
| Compliance and disputes | Consolidated work | KYB/AML/RFI/dispute work is not free, but it can be organized into one workflow instead of scattered across offices and vendors. |

## How the technology creates the savings

1. **Smart Agreement Record (SAR).** The contract, budget, allowed spend
   categories, card pool, residual, hold-back, and public hash live in one
   record instead of separate emails, spreadsheets, checks, and audit folders.
2. **Work Completion Record (WCR).** Payment authorization and compliance proof
   become the same artifact, reducing the later scramble to prove why money
   moved.
3. **Controlled spend cards.** MCC locks and per-card limits turn policy into an
   authorization-time control. A bad category can decline before settlement
   rather than becoming an exception in an audit.
4. **Rail/RLUSD/XRPL record path.** The target rail can move approved money and
   update balances quickly, while XRPL anchors a low-cost public verification
   record. This makes evidence cheap and continuous.
5. **Municipality-owned accounts.** The affiliate model keeps town funds in town
   accounts and makes CivicChain an orchestrator rather than the owner of pooled
   public money. That protects the savings story from commingling risk.

## Why CivicChain Can Beat The Traditional Workflow

Traditional rails can be cheap, but the traditional municipal workflow around
them is expensive. CivicChain wins when the comparison is the whole operating
bundle: approval, payment, controls, reconciliation, audit evidence, and public
explanation.

| Current reality | What it means for CivicChain |
| --- | --- |
| Public card portals often expose residents to 2-3% convenience fees. | The fee pain is visible and easy to understand. |
| ACH/e-check/check paths can be cheap at the rail layer. | CivicChain does not need to win on rail fee alone; it wins on all-in workflow cost. |
| FedNow, RTP, ACH, AP automation, and p-cards each improve pieces of payment. | CivicChain bundles payment, controls, evidence, and buyer-defensible public-record posture into one contract record. |
| Bank analysis statements and treasury services carry lockbox, reporting, Positive Pay, reconciliation, research, return, and exception work. | CivicChain's event record attacks the labor and exception layer. |
| Vendor payments stay slow when warrants, invoices, checks, reconciliation, and records are manual. | This is the core opening: same work, same vendor, same contract, but less payment uncertainty and less evidence labor. |
| Town staff answer audit and public-record questions from fragmented records. | CivicChain makes contract spend self-documenting so staff can answer from the record instead of rebuilding it. |

## Boundaries That Keep The Claim Strong

- CivicChain SaaS fees, payout fees, interchange, and float are company revenue,
  not automatically customer savings. They are justified when the town's all-in
  workflow cost falls by more than CivicChain charges.
- Float yield on public funds is legally contested; do not use it as a customer
  or company benefit without counsel sign-off.
- XRPL's near-zero transaction cost proves cheap anchoring, not that the total
  municipal payment workflow is near-zero cost.
- Lower bids do not automatically become town savings by Maine statute. The
  town-level award rule needs local confirmation.
- Do not make "official sources are lying" the public story. The better business
  story is that posted fee schedules miss labor, exceptions, audit work, and
  payment uncertainty.

## Pilot Proof Ladder

A pilot upgrades the mechanism from modeled to measured by capturing:

- Real measured payment-cycle baseline by target town and contract type.
- All-in cost baseline per reconciled, audit-ready transaction: visible fees,
  processor invoices, bank analysis statements, AP/reconciliation time,
  exception logs, audit adjustments, close-calendar time, and public-record
  request hours.
- Real bid comparison before and after CivicChain, or at least founder/customer
  confirmation that vendors price payment delay into bids.
- Measured clerk/AP/reconciliation hours before and after a pilot.
- Confirmed v1.0 card scope and card-program economics.
- Counsel-reviewed wording for warrant workflow, money transmission, float, and
  town procurement rules.
