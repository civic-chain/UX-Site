# Marketing Content Claim Audit

Source scope:
`/Users/triton/Documents/GitHub/civicchain-marketing/content`.

This file captures useful facts and contradictions from the sibling marketing
content packet. It is an evidence note, not public copy.

## Adopted Into The Local Fact Boundary

- The first story should be municipal contracting, cleaner contractor payments,
  and visible accountability. Blockchain, XRPL, RLUSD, and Rail explain the
  trust architecture after the buyer understands the operational problem.
- `The Stripe for municipal contracting` is a useful investor shorthand, not
  the whole positioning.
- Use explicit proof labels: `actual / observed`, `modeled`, `target`, and
  `needs proof`.
- Harpswell is a demo instance / scenario unless another business-status source
  confirms a live customer, LOI, signed pilot, or design partner.
- Civic-Chain should not hold pooled municipal funds in Civic-Chain-owned
  accounts. The defensible architecture is municipality-owned funds, with
  Civic-Chain orchestrating workflow and payment operations.
- Actions that move money or change the public record need administrator
  approval.
- Work Completion Records are payment and compliance artifacts, not only
  internal task status.
- Approvals and declines can both become audit evidence. A blocked transaction
  is useful proof that contract-bound restrictions were enforced.
- Citizen voting is a jurisdiction-aware civic-input layer. Treat it as a
  differentiator / later layer, not a universal procurement default.
- Stablecoin-native settlement is optional or later-layer. The USD-first story
  must still stand on its own.

## New Or Clarified Product Facts

- Municipality-facing view: contracts, pending actions, refill requests,
  payment approvals, and card activity.
- Vendor-facing view: balances, spend restrictions, residual payment status,
  Work Completion Records, and refill requests.
- Public-facing view: open contract and spend ledger without login.
- Minimum audit layer: solicitation timeline, proposal intake log, voting
  record where enabled, payment authorization log, refill request log.
- Spend control: MCC restrictions, per-card limits, hard decline when the pool
  is exhausted, and refill requests with separate approval.
- Compliance package concept: ledger log, vote confidence trail where enabled,
  vendor screening record, WCR log, and SAR anchor hash.
- Initial service categories in the white paper: snow and ice removal, road
  maintenance, fire protection, public safety, and general public works.
- Rural NFC gap has a product answer in the raw white paper: phone tap is the
  primary flow, with physical backup cards as exception fallback.
