# High-impact landing fast note

Fast memory note, not landing structure and not source truth. Use it to remember
the strongest CivicChain advantages before a landing pass; linked owner docs
keep the exact wording and claim boundaries.

## Highest-impact reminders

1. **Control happens before and during spend, not after the mess.** CivicChain's
   sharpest difference is authorization-time control plus evidence while the
   contract is active. [[_docs/business/1-foundation/benefits-by-audience|Owner]]

2. **The real savings claim is workflow cost, not cheap crypto rails.** The
   useful unit is all-in cost per reconciled, audit-ready municipal transaction:
   vendor uncertainty, staff time, spend control, audit prep, and public-record
   work. [[_docs/business/3-model/customer-savings-mechanism|Owner]]

3. **Public-by-default spending protects the town manager.** If someone asks
   where the money went, the record is already visible instead of rebuilt under
   pressure. [[_docs/facts/product|Owner]]

4. **Small towns are the wedge because the pain is operational.** One or two
   people often carry clerk, treasurer, tax, procurement, records, and IT work
   at once. [[_docs/facts/evidence/maine-municipal-constraints|Owner]]

5. **Vendor speed is a buyer benefit.** Faster board-gated payment can reduce
   payment uncertainty, status-chasing, and the risk premium vendors price into
   bids. [[_docs/business/1-foundation/benefits-by-audience|Owner]]

6. **Residents get self-serve proof instead of a records request.** The public
   interface lets residents inspect contract spend and verify ledger hashes
   without becoming a staff workload. [[_docs/facts/product|Owner]]

7. **The confirmed core is the public contract record.** Smart Agreement
   Records, Work Completion Records, payment authorization flow, burn-rate
   alerts, and exportable reporting are the spine to keep central.
   [[_docs/facts/product|Owner]]

8. **XRPL is useful because it makes verification independent.** The ledger is
   the proof layer underneath municipal contracting, not the opening story.
   [[_docs/business/6-technology/20-xrp-ledger-and-records|Owner]]

9. **Contract-bound card controls are the most concrete "wow" mechanism.** MCC
   locks are standard card infrastructure, but CivicChain ties them to the
   procurement contract and can turn approvals and declines into audit events.
   [[_docs/business/6-technology/22-cards-and-mcc-lock|Owner]]

10. **Faster payment must stay inside the warrant workflow.** The story is
    faster board-gated payment, not bypassing local authority.
    [[_docs/facts/evidence/maine-municipal-constraints|Owner]]

11. **An evidence-only pilot is a strong low-friction entry.** CivicChain can
    prove public-by-default value with warrant uploads, AP exports, and public
    dashboards before asking to move money.
    [[_docs/business/1-foundation/benefits-by-audience|Owner]]

12. **The IP story is real but must stay precise.** Provisional applications
    were filed on April 4, 2026 for cross-ledger audit, contract-bound MCC card
    controls, and geolocation eligibility; do not call them issued patents.
    [[_docs/business/4-execution/11-ip-and-moat|Owner]]

13. **Geolocation voting is cool, but not v1.0.** Keep it as Tier 2 /
    jurisdiction-aware civic input with privacy boundaries, not as the landing's
    core promise. [[_docs/facts/product|Owner]]

14. **The best plain-English contrast is simple.** Today: contractor spends,
    invoices, town reconciles later, residents ask later. CivicChain: contract
    pool, allowed spend, immediate record, public verification.
    [[_docs/facts/product|Owner]]

## Claim guardrails

- Lead with cleaner contracting, faster approved payment, controlled spend, and
  visible accountability. Keep blockchain underneath as the verification method.
- Treat card controls, same-day settlement, RLUSD, geolocation voting, and dollar
  savings with their source claim labels before turning them into public copy.
