# Audience fears and wants

The content foundation for the landing page. One question, two audiences:
**what does each visitor fear, and what do they want, the moment they meet a
project like CivicChain?**

The page speaks *to* the decision-maker (the **town manager**) but is read *over
his shoulder* by ordinary people — residents, the contractors he hires, the
workers who carry the cards. Every line has to sell the manager without
frightening the people watching. This document maps both sides so the copy can
answer real fears instead of inventing them.

It is derived from `_docs/business/`. It is **not** a new source of truth: where
a reassurance leans on a product capability, that capability keeps the canon's
claim label.

If a reassurance touches an unresolved founder, counsel, or product decision,
route it through [[_docs/business/open-external-blockers|Open external blockers]]
instead of smoothing it into marketing copy.

`_docs/facts/Narrative Research.pdf` adds a messaging correction, not new
product canon: the landing story should stay buyer-first. Resident, vendor, and
worker stories are proof points for the municipal buyer, not co-equal sales
messages. A beneficiary benefit belongs on the page only when it translates back
into buyer payoff: lower risk, less staff work, more defensible decisions, a
wider bidder pool, or a cleaner audit / public-record posture.

For the positive "who wins and what they get" owner, use
[[_docs/business/1-foundation/benefits-by-audience|Benefits by audience]]. This
file stays focused on the fear / desire translation that makes landing copy
land without overclaiming.

## The law over every message: outcome first, blockchain underneath

Before any persona, one rule from the Maine evidence governs *how* we answer
every fear:

- **Never lead with "blockchain."** Maine has no documented production
  blockchain pilots in local government, and selling the technology will not
  work. Lead with the **outcome** — "you won't be the next Anson, your auditor
  won't quit, your clerk won't lose Friday to FOAA" — and keep the technology
  underneath, as the answer to *how* trust is verified, not the opening line.
  ([[_docs/business/4-execution/13-risks-and-mitigations|13 — Risks]], "Positioning constraint")
- **Optional, never a mandate.** The municipal-association identity is home rule
  and anti-mandate. Frame everything as voluntary capacity-building the town
  controls on its own timeline — never a state-pushed standard.
- **Promise only what ships.** v1.0 confirmed core: public ledger, Smart
  Agreement Records on XRPL, Work Completion Records, payment authorization,
  burn-rate alerts, exportable reporting. MCC-locked cards, same-day/seconds
  settlement, RLUSD, and citizen voting are **Target** / unresolved — do not let
  a fear-answer present them as shipped.

Claim labels used below are the canon's: **Actual** (observed/confirmed),
**Modeled** (scenario economics), **Target** (roadmap intent),
**Needs proof** (needs a dated external source). Defined in
[[_docs/business/index|the business index]].

# Part A — The decision-maker: the town manager

**Who he is.** One person doing five jobs — treasurer, tax collector, clerk,
procurement officer, and IT — in a town under 25,000, often under 5,000. There
is no budget and no local pipeline to "hire more finance staff." He cannot move
vendor money alone: a part-time select board must vote and sign the warrant, a
structural two-to-four-week cycle (30-A §5603). So he buys the pilot as one
person (§2636), but he operates *inside* the board's authority, and he answers
to residents at town meeting.
([[_docs/business/2-market/05-customers-and-beachhead|05 — Customers]],
[[_docs/business/4-execution/13-risks-and-mitigations|13 — Risks]])

## What he fears

| In his head | Grounded in | How the page can honestly answer |
|---|---|---|
| "I'll be the next embezzlement headline." | Documented Maine pattern — the Anson / Viles case ($500,948, found exactly when the town added payment-tracking software); Sorrento fuel-card case. The fear is real and local. (**Actual** pattern) | The public, immutable record makes concealment structurally hard. Lead with "you won't be the next Anson," not with the ledger tech. Frame: "fraud impossible to *conceal* by design" (**Target** positioning — design argument, not an audited property). |
| "My auditor will quit and I won't pass my Yellow Book audit." | Auditor supply collapsed — 7 of 16 Maine counties behind on audits, annual post-audit still mandatory (30-A §5823). (**Actual**) | "Audit-ready by Friday": the console generates the compliance report package instead of weeks of manual prep. (**Target** — confirmed core story for v1.0, not yet a live deployment.) |
| "This becomes a *second* system and double data entry." | CivicChain does **not** sync with Harris TRIO (the ERP ~475/488 Maine towns run) or InforME PayPort — CSV/PDF export only. For a one-person office that is real added work, carried as Risk #10. (**Actual** constraint) | Do not hide it. Honest frame: compensating controls and self-serve transparency are worth the duplicate entry past the pilot — but that the value outweighs the cost is **Needs proof**. Don't claim integration we don't have. |
| "It'll look like I'm bypassing the select board's power over the warrant." | Vendor money moves only on a board-signed warrant (§5603); *State v. Huff* makes paying without one improper. Boards resist anything that looks like a bypass. (**Actual**) | The product sits **inside** the warrant workflow — digital warrant packets, e-signature, threshold approvals — collapsing the cycle to days, not removing the board. "Faster board-gated payment," never "instant, around the board." |
| "Spending taxpayer money on crypto makes me look reckless." | No Maine blockchain pilots; "blockchain" as a pitch fails; home-rule, anti-mandate politics. (**Actual**) | Outcome-first framing (above). The page is about cleaner contracting and visible accountability; the ledger is the quiet mechanism, never the headline. |
| "Another complex IT thing dumped on my one-person office." | KYB onboarding 3–10 days; adoption resistance is Risk #10; the operator is assumed non-expert. | Capability transfer on the town's timeline — CivicChain admin runs everything at first; the manager sees a dashboard, not blockchain or rail mechanics. (**Target** — design intent.) |

## What he wants

| What he's after | Grounded in | Honesty note |
|---|---|---|
| Political cover — when someone asks "where did the road money go?", the answer is already online. No defensive scramble, no FOIA turnaround. | [[_docs/business/5-journeys/15-journey-town-manager|15 — Town manager]], "political protection" | **Actual** as positioning; strength in a contested case is **Needs proof** (legal/audit). |
| His clerk stops losing Fridays to records requests. | MMA: ~36 "beyond normal effort" requests/yr, most about vendor payments and budget-to-actual — exactly what a live dashboard makes self-serve. (**Actual**) | The strongest, cleanest "want" — real-time expenditure dashboard answers the most common request type. |
| One console instead of email threads, spreadsheets, and paper checks. | [[_docs/business/1-foundation/01-what-it-is|01 — What it is]], three interfaces | **Actual** as product story / **Target** as shipping. |
| Lower bids because vendors stop pricing in payment risk. | Modeled 3–8% risk premium; ~5% bid-drop example. | **Modeled** — do not assert as measured savings, and drop the old "the statute forces lower bids" line (§124 is *county* purchasing — **Needs proof** at the town level). |
| To stay in control — adopt voluntarily, expand on his own schedule. | Phased capability transfer; home-rule framing. | **Target** / positioning. Reinforces "optional, never a mandate." |

# Part B — The ordinary people watching

Three non-buyer personas can land on the page. Their fears and wants matter
because the manager's whole value proposition — "spending is public" — only pays
off if these people trust it, use it, and don't feel policed by it.

## B1 — The resident / taxpayer

**Who she is.** A townsperson, a reporter, "anyone." No login, no standing in
the process — just someone who wants to know where her taxes went.

**Fears**

| In her head | Grounded in | Page answer |
|---|---|---|
| "I can't find out where my money went without filing FOIA and waiting weeks." | Today's reality in product materials; FOAA acknowledgment within 5 working days, deep requests 5+ staff hours. (**Actual** problem framing) | Open the public ledger — no login, no request — see every contract, vendor, transaction. (**Actual** as the transparency story.) |
| (Unstated, ours) "Is this real, or a pretty dashboard the town controls?" | The verifiability story: every record anchored on XRPL, checkable by anyone. | "No trust required" — tap the hash, verify it yourself on a public explorer. (**Target** for the full dashboard; underlying anchoring is **Actual** as product story.) |
| (Unstated, ours) "Is someone tracking *me*?" | Voting-layer privacy architecture: raw GPS discarded, only a boolean + zone + confidence stored; votes anonymous. (**Target**, Tier 2) | Only relevant if the page shows the civic-input layer — keep it framed as advisory and privacy-first, not surveillance. |

**Wants**

- See every active contract, vendor, transaction, and a hash she can verify
  independently — transparency as the default state, not a request. (**Actual**
  as product story.)
- Confidence that the town isn't hiding anything — verifiability without having
  to trust anyone.

**Honesty gap to respect in copy.** The canon documents a reason for the
*first* visit and **no hook for a second** — every alert fires to the manager,
not to residents, and voting is Tier 2 and advisory
([[_docs/business/5-journeys/18-journey-resident|18 — Resident]], "What brings a
resident back"). Do **not** write copy that promises an engaged, returning civic
community we can't yet deliver. Sell the open door, not a daily habit.

## B2 — The vendor / contractor

**Who he is.** A small contractor on seasonal work — plowing, roads,
landscaping — in a town under 25K. The defining fact of his life today: he waits
**30–45 days** to be paid and quietly finances that wait by padding every bid.

**Fears**

| In his head | Grounded in | Page answer |
|---|---|---|
| "I finish the work and wait a month or more to get paid." | [[_docs/business/5-journeys/16-journey-vendor|16 — Vendor]] (30–45 day baseline is **Needs proof** — narrative claim, not measured). | Paid faster once the disbursement is authorized — but **board-gated, not instant**. Honest promise: "days, not a month," never "same day, guaranteed." |
| "A locked card will decline at the worst moment, mid-job." | MCC-lock; Risk #7 — older rural pump terminals lack tap; the demo showed *approvals*, never a decline. (**Target**, card scope unresolved) | Physical backup card with the same pool/locks as a fallback. Do not over-promise the card mechanism — its v1.0 scope is unresolved. |
| "The pool runs dry and I have to beg for a refill before I can keep working." | Hard-decline + admin-approved refill is a deliberate checkpoint (Risk #9). | This is a *feature for the town*, friction for him. Frame the upside (predictable budget) honestly; don't pretend the checkpoint is for his benefit. |
| "More paperwork just to bid." | Structured proposal data model, bonds, insurance (**Target**). | A standardized, side-by-side proposal process — frame as fairness and speed, not extra burden. |

**Wants**

- Get paid fast and stop fronting the town's float. (**Modeled**/Target.)
- Stop padding bids with a risk premium — win more by bidding true. (**Modeled**.)
- Zero invoices, zero end-of-month statements. (**Target**.)

## B3 — The worker / driver

**Who he is.** The crew member who actually taps the card at the pump and the
supply yard. The sharpest persona, because the system is **imposed on him** — and
the canon names **no benefit to him at all**
([[_docs/business/5-journeys/17-journey-worker|17 — Worker]], "What's in it for
the worker").

**Fears**

| In his head | Grounded in | Page answer |
|---|---|---|
| "They don't trust me — locked card, every tap logged, named to me, biometric." | Named Worker Card, per-tap XRPL record, biometric tap-to-pay. (**Target**) | The page mostly speaks *past* the worker to the manager and vendor. Don't pretend he loves the control. If addressed at all: less personal cash fronted, no expense reports — but that benefit is **unconfirmed** (not in canon). |
| "The card declines at a rural pump and I'm stranded." | Risk #7, NFC gaps. | Physical backup card. (**Target**.) |

**Wants** — *none confirmed in canon.* The closest implied benefit (no fronting
personal cash, no expense paperwork) is **unconfirmed** and must not be asserted
as fact. **Content implication:** the worker is the weakest-motivated link and
sets the adoption ceiling for anything that depends on field staff actually
tapping. Treat him as a risk to acknowledge internally, not a persona to court on
the page.

## Cross-cutting rules for whoever writes the copy

1. **Outcome first, blockchain underneath** — every persona, every section.
2. **Buyer payoff first, beneficiary proof underneath.** Resident transparency,
   vendor payment speed, and worker usability are useful only when they make the
   town's decision safer, cheaper, easier to defend, or easier to operate.
3. **Optional and town-controlled** — never a mandate, never state-pushed.
4. **Promise only v1.0 confirmed core.** Public ledger, SAR, WCR, payment
   authorization, burn-rate alerts, exportable reporting are available. MCC cards,
   same-day/seconds settlement, RLUSD, and voting are **Target** — frame as the
   roadmap, not today.
5. **Do not import unreconciled tier, ROI, legal, or pricing language.** Marketing
   copy consumes the business index and claim owners, not raw source packets.
   Unresolved external blockers stay unresolved; copy can route around them, not
   solve them.
6. **Two honesty gaps that will tempt over-promising:** the resident's missing
   *second visit*, and the worker's missing *incentive*. Write around them, don't
   paper over them.
7. **Drop the dead statutory hook** — "Maine law forces lower bids (§124)" is
   **Needs proof** at the town level; lower bids come through buyer choice and
   visible public-record pressure, not an assured mandate.
8. **Dollar savings copy is modeled.** If a page needs numbers, consume
   [[_docs/business/3-model/customer-savings-dollar-model|Customer savings dollar model]]
   and keep the label **Modeled / Bold internal scenario**. Do not present its
   numbers as audited ROI.
