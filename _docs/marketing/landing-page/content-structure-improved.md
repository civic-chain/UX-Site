# Landing content structure — improved

The "after" half of the pair. It reshapes the current page
([[content-structure-current]]) around the audience map
([[audience-fears-and-wants]]) and the strongest advantages
([[high-impact-landing-claims]]).

**This is a target spec, not an instruction to rewrite the live page.** Live
landing copy is in the untouchable layer (AGENTS.md), and the current stage is
visual cleanup before the client preview. Applying any of this to
`app/(site)/**` is a separate, explicit decision. This direction supersedes the
savings-led [[content-structure-speed-and-savings]] variant as the recommended
shape.

## The positioning, in one place

The page is built on one tagline that does three jobs at once — it is the spine
every section hangs from:

> **Every public dollar — decided, spent, and proven in the open.**
> *The accountability the big systems sell — without the enterprise overhead.
> Start at $0.*

It evolves the live hero ("Every dollar on the record") by widening "on the
record" (one slot: proof) into **three slots — decided / spent / proven** — so
every CivicChain capability has a home on the line instead of falling off the
page as a footnote. Section 2 makes those three words the page's visual backbone:

- **Decided** in the open — the award and its basis are public. *(Deepens later
  into geolocation citizen voting — Target / Tier 2.)*
- **Spent** in the open — every transaction lands on the public ledger.
  *(Deepens into contract-bound card control and faster settlement — Target.)*
- **Proven** — tamper-evident record, audit package, exports. *(Confirmed v1.0
  core.)*

The complete differentiator is **control + proof**: the money can only go where
it was approved, *and* anyone can prove it did. Transparency alone does not
differentiate — incumbents claim accountability too. Control before/during
spend is the sharpest difference ([[high-impact-landing-claims]] #1), even
though it leans on card scope that is **Target** for v1.0.

The buyer is the **town manager**; residents, vendors, and workers read over his
shoulder and appear only as proof, never as co-equal sales messages.

### Beachhead, not ceiling

Small towns are the **beachhead** (who we win first — one-person office,
one-person pilot decision, weak incumbents), not the market ceiling. The plan
runs to mid-cities, counties, and states
([[_docs/business/3-model/09-go-to-market|four-phase rollout]]). So the page:

- **Broadens the promise** — affordability is the universal draw (a city
  overpaying an incumbent wants it as much as a town of 3,000). Lead the value,
  not the size.
- **Softens the label** — elastic "your town / what you actually need," not
  "small town," in the hero.
- **Keeps the proof beachhead-vivid** — the one-person office and the $0
  one-person pilot are the converting details; frame them as the *entry point*,
  not the ceiling.

## Disciplines that govern every line

1. **Fear is the private target; copy is calm relief.** Answer the manager's
   fears (scandal, audit, FOAA, looking reckless) as relief, never accusation.
   Never the word "guilty," never the corruption frame — *"the problem is
   infrastructure, not corruption."* ([[audience-fears-and-wants]], the
   positioning law.)
2. **Beneficiaries are proof, not parallel buyers.** Vendor speed, resident
   transparency, and worker usability appear only when they translate into buyer
   payoff: lower risk, fewer records requests, broader bidder pool, cleaner
   audit posture, decisions easier to defend.
3. **Outcome first, blockchain underneath.** Lead with cleaner contracting,
   controlled spend, faster approved payment, and visible accountability. XRPL
   is the verification method, never the opening line.
4. **Promise only what ships.** Confirmed v1.0 core: public ledger, Smart
   Agreement Records, Work Completion Records, payment authorization, burn-rate
   alerts, exportable reporting. **Target / roadmap:** MCC-locked cards,
   seconds-level settlement, RLUSD, geolocation voting — frame as where it goes
   (§6), never as today.
5. **Affordability is value, not "cheapest."** Use *"without the enterprise
   overhead / priced for what you actually need"* (canon-backed,
   [[_docs/business/1-foundation/01-what-it-is|01 — What it is]]). Do **not**
   write "cheaper than competitors" or "the cheapest" — competitor pricing is
   **Needs proof**, ours is **Modeled**, and a price-war superlative is
   politically risky for a public-money buyer. Competitor references stay
   directional positioning, never a benchmarked claim.
6. **Dollar claims are Modeled.** Savings numbers come from
   [[_docs/business/3-model/customer-savings-dollar-model|Customer savings dollar model]]
   as **Modeled / bold internal scenario**, never audited ROI.
7. **Optional and town-controlled.** Voluntary capacity the town adopts on its
   own timeline; never a state-pushed mandate.

## Proposed section flow

Spine of the arc: **Promise → (pain + solution, in three) → Relief → Proof →
Explore → Vision → Trust → Objections → Ask.** Real/today is front-loaded
(1–5); the ambitious roadmap is shown openly but later (6); credibility and
close follow (7–9).

| #   | Section                      | Primary job                                                            | Carries                                                    |
| --- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Hero**                     | Promise + access + protection                                          | tagline · access subline · control-proof animation         |
| 2   | **Decided · Spent · Proven** | The spine made visual — problem *and* how, in three before→after cards | the three pillars, each a today→CivicChain story           |
| 3   | **For the town office**      | Turn three fears into relief                                           | nothing to hide · audit-ready · clerk's Friday back        |
| 4   | **Why it matters**           | Prove it is worth it                                                   | two-tier Modeled savings · cited stats                     |
| 5   | **See it from three sides**  | Proof by exploration                                                   | admin / vendor / public demo views                         |
| 6   | **Where this goes**          | Show the cool roadmap, honestly                                        | voting · cards · faster settlement (Today/Coming)          |
| 7   | **Trust strip**              | Credibility                                                            | patents (provisional) · own accounts · public verification |
| 8   | **FAQ**                      | Remove last objections                                                 | five answers + replace-my-system + bypass-my-board         |
| 9   | **CTA**                      | The manager's next step                                                | transparency-first pilot · $0 · approve it yourself        |

## Section-by-section direction

Copy below is **direction with example lines**, not final copy — calm-relief
register, claim-honest. Final wording stays close to the client's voice if/when
this is applied.

### 1. Hero

- **Headline:** "Every public dollar — decided, spent, and proven in the open."
- **Subline:** "The accountability the big systems sell — without the enterprise
  overhead. Start at $0." *(Access-forward and size-agnostic; "big systems" is
  directional positioning, not a benchmarked claim.)*
- **Visual / animation:** a contract card **declines an out-of-scope charge**,
  and a dollar **lands on the public ledger in real time** as it is spent — the
  most concrete "wow" ([[high-impact-landing-claims]] #9). Control + proof in one
  motion. *(Card control is **Target**; render as the product story, and keep the
  ledger anchoring — which is core — as the load-bearing half.)*

### 2. Decided · Spent · Proven — the spine made visual

Three big-word cards that turn the tagline into the page's backbone. Each card
is a short **before → after** story, so this one section does the work of both a
problem statement (the *Today* line) and a how-it-works (the *With CivicChain*
line). Lead-with-solution risk: the *Today* lines must do real work, or the
cards lose their tension.

**DECIDED**
> Today, who won the contract — and why — lives in someone's inbox.
> With CivicChain, the award and the reasoning go public the moment the decision
> is made — so the choice defends itself.
> *→ Coming: residents weigh in, location-verified and anonymous.* **(Target / Tier 2.)**

**SPENT**
> Today, the contractor spends, invoices, and the town reconciles weeks later.
> With CivicChain, every dollar is released against the contract and lands on the
> public ledger as it happens — inside your board's approval, never around it.
> *→ Coming: a contract card that declines out-of-scope spend.* **(Target.)**

**PROVEN**
> Today, when someone asks where the money went, you rebuild the answer by hand,
> on a deadline.
> With CivicChain, the record assembles itself — tamper-evident, exportable,
> already public. The proof is ready before the question.
> *(Confirmed v1.0 core — no roadmap tag; it stays clean and confident.)*

Honesty folded in: **Spent** carries the warrant truth ("inside your board's
approval, never around it") — payment is faster *once approved*, **days not a
month, never instant**. The TRIO "export, not integration" point lives in the
FAQ (§8), not here.

### 3. For the town office — the relief

The emotional layer the functional cards above don't carry. Three relief cards,
each a private fear turned into calm public language:

- **Nothing to hide, by default** *(fear: a scandal on my watch)* — "Every
  contract and payment sits on an open record. Transparency is the state of
  things, not a request you answer under pressure." **Actual** as the
  transparency story.
- **Audit-ready without the all-nighter** *(fear: the auditor, the Yellow Book)*
  — "The record assembles the contract trail as you go, so audit prep stops being
  weeks of compilation." Frame as **designed to**, not "your audit is done."
- **Your clerk's Friday back** *(fear: FOAA Fridays)* — "A live ledger answers
  the most common request before anyone files." The strongest, cleanest relief.

*Do not* lean this section on the card ("unauthorized spending impossible") —
card scope is **Target** for v1.0.

### 4. Why it matters — the cost of the old way

**Headline:** "Friction has a price — your town is already paying it." Master
line: *"Cheap rail, expensive workflow"* — the value is in the process around the
payment, not a cheaper rail.

- **Two tiers** so the page speaks past small towns: a tiny town (~$385K spend →
  **~$25K/year**) and a mid-size municipality (~$5M spend → **~$115K/year, six
  figures**). Both **Modeled / bold internal scenario** — label them, never
  audited ROI.
- **Cited external stats** (keep, properly attributed): **$208B** late-payment
  cost (Rabbet), **~25%** procurement loss (World Bank), **82% faster** with
  digital workflows (Ardent).

Never "cheaper than banks," never "guaranteed savings."

### 5. See it from three sides — the demo

**Headline:** "Open the same contract from every side." Town staff (`/admin`) —
run it · Contractor (`/vendor`) — get paid, see allowed spend · Public
(`/citizen`) — verify with no login. The public view *is* "proven in the open"
you can touch. **Placed below the buyer argument** — proof by exploration, not
three co-equal front doors.

### 6. Where this goes — the roadmap, shown honestly

The block that lets us show the unique, cool capabilities **without hiding them
and without a false present-tense claim.** A clear **Today / Coming** split,
deepening each tagline word:

- **Decided →** geolocation-verified **citizen voting** — residents weigh in,
  verified by location, **anonymous and privacy-first**. *(Coming · Tier 2 ·
  advisory, not a binding vendor-selection mechanism.)*
- **Spent →** **contract-bound cards** that decline out-of-scope charges, and
  **seconds-level settlement** so approved payment lands fast. *(Coming ·
  Target.)*
- **Proven →** already the shipping core.

Add one ambition line so the page does not read small-only: *"From a town of
2,000 to a city of 200,000."* Frame as roadmap/vision, not "we serve big cities
today" (Phase 1 is small towns).

### 7. Trust strip — credibility

Three signals: **patent-pending architecture** (provisional applications filed
April 4, 2026 — never "issued patents" or "patented technology") · **your town
keeps its money in its own accounts** (never "we hold the money") ·
**independently verifiable** on a public ledger.

### 8. FAQ — keep five, add two

Keep the five live answers (who it's for, why small towns, not-a-blockchain-
company, public input optional, why payment clarity matters). **Add the two
adoption objections the manager actually carries:**

- **"Do I have to replace the system I already use?"** — "No. It runs alongside
  your current books; you export the record into them." *(Honest: no TRIO
  integration — export, a real second-entry cost, not a rip-and-replace.)*
- **"Does this go around my select board?"** — "No. It works inside your warrant
  and approval process; the board's authority stays where it is."

### 9. CTA — the manager's next step

**Headline:** "See it on your own contracts." The **transparency-first pilot** —
**start at $0**, prove the public-record value before moving a dollar. For a
small town, the pilot is **approvable by one person** (under the Town Manager's
authority, Maine 30-A §2636 — no town meeting); for a bigger municipality this
softens to *"a low-cost pilot you can start fast."*

## Claim flags (carry into any copy pass)

- **Target / roadmap** — MCC cards, seconds-level settlement, RLUSD, geolocation
  voting. Show as "where it goes" (§6), never as shipping today.
- **Modeled** — all dollar-savings numbers. Label them; never audited ROI.
- **Provisional only** — patents. Never "issued" or "patented technology."
- **Needs proof** — "cheaper than competitors / the cheapest." Off the page; use
  value-for-what-you-need instead.
- **Honest constraints** — no TRIO integration (export, not sync); payment is
  faster *inside* the warrant, never instant; funds stay in the town's accounts.

## Remaining decisions for the owner

1. **Validate the spear with a real manager.** The emotional center — relief from
   *exposure* (burden of proof) vs relief from *exhaustion* (sole-bearer
   overwhelm) — is our best reading of the evidence, not lived truth. One
   conversation with a target-town manager (Gray, Camden, Kennebunk) should
   confirm which nerve to press before the visual identity commits to it.
2. **How far to soften the beachhead.** This spec broadens the *promise* (price,
   universal) while keeping the *proof* (one-person office, $0 pilot)
   beachhead-specific. Going broader still (speaking to all sizes equally) would
   dilute the Phase-1 converting wedge — a real trade, the owner's call.
3. **How direct the relief section gets** — calm relief (recommended) vs naming
   the stakes more bluntly. Bluntness risks insulting a prospect.
4. **Scope of this spec** — a target for later, or a brief to apply to live copy
   now? Live copy is untouchable without explicit scope, and we are mid visual
   cleanup before the client preview.
