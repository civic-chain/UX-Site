# Contact Page

## Verdict

mixed

## Full Visual Critique

1. A jury would say: neat, but too utilitarian and under-composed for competition-level design. Evidence: strong heading exists, but below it are large simple slabs without expressive composition.
2. First impression: the eye lands on `Pilot conversation preview`, then falls into emptiness and gets stuck on the large green card. Evidence: there is a huge gap between hero and first slab, and the card carries the main visual weight.
3. Meaning: the page wants to say "this is an easy path to a pilot conversation." It communicates that clearly, but dryly. Evidence: text directly says low-friction path, but visually it feels more like a placeholder.
4. Main action: email is visible, but action does not dominate. Evidence: the `info@...` button sits far below on the right, while the top screen sells description before contact.
5. Squint hierarchy: heading first, green slab second, email CTA third. Hierarchy is almost right, but CTA is weak. Evidence: the button is small relative to the green block's mass.
6. Air: there is a lot of air, but it reads as accidental emptiness, not luxury. Evidence: the upper half of the screen is almost empty on the right and between hero/card.
7. Section connection: the three parts connect by color, but rhythm breaks. Evidence: hero is compact, green section is wide and heavy, email slab is thin and stretched again.
8. Odd forms: the email block is too stretched and low. Evidence: the blue slab spans nearly full width but contains only one short phrase and button.
9. Odd empty spaces: the main gap is the empty right side of hero and the large space before the green block. Evidence: the top-right sector has no compositional hold.
10. Interface clarity is there, but `Back to landing` feels like an extra action on the contact page. Evidence: the hero button competes with the future contact action.
11. Visual material: no illustrations, no icons, almost no decor; the page looks poorer than it could. Evidence: the whole visual language depends on colored rectangles and radii.
12. Alignment: left edge is good, but the right side stays empty until the CTA. Evidence: almost all semantic text sits left; balance appears only in the lower right button.
13. Spacing: internal spacing is pleasant, external gaps are too loose. Evidence: inside the green card everything is even, but section distance is excessive.
14. Text: not overloaded, but green-section lines are slightly long and dull. Evidence: the paragraph stretches into two calm lines without a strong accent.
15. Freshness: more template than authored. Evidence: large pastel cards, pill elements, and blue CTA feel like a standard modern template.
16. It needs to become more precise and slightly bolder. Evidence: trust already exists through calm colors, but there is no strong visual "invitation to talk."
17. Gestalt problem: weak proximity between goal and action. Evidence: "start here" meaning lives in the green section, while the real contact is separated by a large vertical gap.
18. One main fix: bring hero, explanation, and email into one more cohesive contact composition. Evidence: right now three sections read as separate slabs, not one convincing path to demo/pilot.

## Three-Section Context

The story is clear: preview -> why to write -> where to write. Visually, though, it is broken apart. Hero feels like a temporary placeholder, green section like an info card, email block like a separate banner. To make this one marketing page, reduce gaps, make CTA the main continuation of the story, and add one expressive visual motif: for example, a refined "pilot path" composition, route line, or stronger contact block.

## What To Improve

- Reduce vertical gaps and bring hero + green block into one composition.
- Strengthen email CTA: larger, closer to the meaning text, with clearer status as the main action.
- Replace three equally stretched slabs with livelier rhythm: one strong central card + compact final CTA, not two wide banners in a row.

## Main Fix

Turn the page from a utilitarian placeholder into a short cohesive route to a pilot conversation.
