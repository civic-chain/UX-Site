# About Page

## Verdict

promising

## Full Visual Critique

1. A jury would say: the page is neat, calm, and tasteful, but not yet competition-level. Evidence: the large blue serif heading feels confident, but the team cards look too much like template text tiles without a strong visual image.
2. First impression: the eye lands on the heading, then gets stuck on the three cards, especially Daniel's yellow card. Evidence: it is the warmest and most contrast-heavy card, so it pulls more attention than the others.
3. Meaning: "the team is competent, mature, and trustworthy." It achieves this at about 7/10. Evidence: roles and experience read clearly, but the lack of faces, photos, or a more vivid team signal makes trust abstract.
4. Main action is moderately clear: trust the team and write the email. Evidence: CTA exists below, but it is visually quiet and far from the cards; the email button is visible but does not feel like the page climax.
5. Squint hierarchy: heading first, three colored cards second, CTA third. Overall hierarchy is right. Evidence: heading size and large cards keep the order, but the CTA is too low and too horizontally stretched.
6. There is plenty of air, but some of it reads as accidental emptiness. Evidence: the large gap between hero text and cards does not create drama; it simply breaks the page.
7. The three sections connect, but weakly: hero, team, and CTA look like neatly placed blocks, not one story. Evidence: hero has no visual bridge to cards, and CTA lives separately in color and shape.
8. Odd forms exist: CTA is too long and flat. Evidence: the green slab stretches almost full width while containing only one line and a button.
9. Odd emptiness exists: the main gap is between hero text and cards. Evidence: the upper half of the page breathes too widely, while content is gathered lower in a dense row.
10. The interface is familiar and readable, but card weight differences are jarring. Evidence: white, blue, and yellow cards feel like three levels of importance even though people should read as one team.
11. Visual material fits cleanly but feels thin. Evidence: there are only initials circles and colored cards; no portraits, team image, subtle graphics, or trust-strengthening detail.
12. Alignment is generally good, but internal card rhythm differs. Evidence: Daniel's heading sits optically lower than Justin's and Michael's, making the third card feel like it sagged.
13. Spacing errors: too much vertical gap after hero and too much gap before CTA. Evidence: cards feel like they float separately between top text and lower slab.
14. The text is not overloaded, but cards are dense. Evidence: bios run in small lines, especially Michael and Daniel; they want slightly stronger structure.
15. It feels partly fresh, but there is a template AI-design scent. Evidence: three large cards with initials, pastel backgrounds, and a cream background are a very recognizable pattern.
16. The block needs to become more precise, not bolder. Evidence: GovTech audience needs calm confidence; the issue is not quietness but loose composition and weak team image.
17. Gestalt conflict: team elements do not read as one equal-status group. Evidence: different card colors create competition, not one system; the yellow card looks highlighted even though the meaning does not say "main person."
18. One main fix: align the cards as one team system and remove accidental air gaps. Evidence: beauty breaks not in font details, but in overall placement: hero far above, team separate, CTA separate.

## Three-Section Context

Hero sets a beautiful, confident tone. Cards continue the meaning but become more utilitarian and template-like. CTA closes the page logically, but it is too flat and stretched, so the ending does not feel like a refined final point.

For the page to hold as one marketing page, hero needs to connect more tightly to the team, cards need more equal treatment, and the trust accent needs to feel more intentional.

## What To Improve

- Reduce the vertical gap between hero and cards so the page feels more gathered.
- Bring cards into one system: less difference in color strength, consistent internal rhythm, and no visual sag in Daniel's card.
- Make CTA more compact and expressive: less "flat strip," more final invitation to contact.

## Main Fix

Unify hero, team cards, and CTA into one trust story where the team reads as an equal group.
